# Research for an Understandable Script

## Build a source brief

Record the audience's starting knowledge, the question to answer, and the concrete situation that makes the question matter. List the few facts needed to follow the causal explanation, including familiar facts that provide essential context.

For an implementation-based video, distinguish:
- Behavior verified in code or deterministic tests.
- Behavior observed with a real system or model, with its coverage limits.
- An illustrative conversation or simplified scenario.
- A design option that has not been implemented or tested.

Keep sources in production notes. Do not turn a controlled test prompt into purported natural user dialogue. Preserve the limits of the test when simplifying its presentation. Never infer that all models or platforms behave alike from one observation.

## Research only what the script needs

Read supplied material first. Inspect primary documentation, code, or reports for uncertain consequential claims. Competitor videos can suggest presentation questions but are not evidence for technical behavior. Do not download transcripts or run keyword research as an automatic prerequisite.

For discovery, entertainment, or independent list items, novelty can help choose material. A subjective novelty score is an editorial aid, not evidence or a mandatory quota. For teaching, retain a familiar fact when it supplies a prerequisite or resolves likely confusion.

If title or SEO work is requested, use verified information and relevant keywords. Do not invent traffic figures, tool results, or performance guarantees.

## Brief output

- Audience and assumed prior knowledge.
- Learning promise in one sentence.
- Natural running example and the context it needs.
- Concept sequence and supporting evidence.
- Unknowns, proposals, and details to leave in notes.
